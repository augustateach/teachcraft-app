Cambridge C1 Advanced · Reading Part 5

Reading — The Friend Effect: Reading Between the Lines

A reading-led lesson on writer purpose, implication and evidence-based inference.

C1 Advanced Approx. 60 minutes Student + Teacher modes
Learning objective: By the end of this lesson, you will be able to identify a writer’s purpose and interpret implied meaning in a long C1-level article, using precise textual evidence to justify your answers.
Activate

Speaking — Warm-up

Answer the question with a partner. Give a developed answer rather than a simple yes/no response.

How important is spending time face-to-face with friends to you, compared with staying in touch online — and why?

Teacher note: Elicit two or three ideas before pair work. Do not pre-teach the reading vocabulary at this point.
Notice and practise

Word Formation

Use the word in capitals to form a word that fits each gap. Write only the missing word.

For decades, doctors focused mainly on diet and exercise when discussing physical health. Today, however, researchers argue that social (1) __________ deserves just as much attention. Adults who feel chronically (2) __________ face a (3) __________ higher risk of serious illness, and the effect can be as damaging as smoking. What seems to matter most is not the quantity of contact but its quality: a short, (4) __________ conversation can do more for wellbeing than hours spent scrolling through a feed. Psychologists also stress the value of (5) __________ listening — being fully present for a friend rather than simply waiting to speak. People with a small circle of close, (6) __________ friends tend to report far greater (7) __________ with life than those with hundreds of loose online contacts. In short, real closeness cannot be (8) __________ by convenience.

Teaching link: Monitor the vocabulary of connection, isolation, reliability and authenticity; it will return in the reading and transfer stages.
Controlled reformulation

Key Word Transformations

Complete each sentence using three to six words, including the key word. Do not change the key word.
Bridge to reading

Before You Read

You have explored how friendship may affect wellbeing. The article now examines the issue through research and expert opinion.

Headline prediction

Look only at the headline and subheading:

“The friend effect: why the secret of health and happiness is surprisingly simple.”

What might the writer mean by a “secret” that is “surprisingly simple”? Note two possibilities.

Teacher note: Accept any reasonable prediction. Do not confirm or reject ideas yet.
Global understanding

First Reading

Read the whole article once without stopping to check vocabulary. Then choose the best summary.
Conditions: Limit the first reading to 3–4 minutes. No dictionaries or re-reading. The goal is fluency and gist.

Make the process visible

How to Justify an Answer

1. Locate
Find the paragraph named or implied by the question.
2. Prove
Underline the exact words supporting your chosen option.
3. Eliminate
Cross out options that are plausible in general but unsupported by this text.
Rule: If you cannot underline evidence for an option, it is not a defensible answer.

Reflection on one item

Ask “Why is option X wrong?” for at least two questions. The elimination process is part of the lesson objective, not merely answer checking.
Notice and reuse

Language Mining

Find each expression in the article, infer its meaning from context, then rewrite it in your own words.
The aim is productive reuse. Do not turn this into a dictionary-definition exercise.
Produce

Communicative Transfer

Discuss in pairs, using at least two expressions from the previous section:

Gilbert argues that social media has made society “plastic” and left people feeling inadequate. Do you agree? What could someone realistically do this week to build one more genuine, face-to-face connection?

Monitor for purposeful reuse of the mined language. Ask students to underline the expressions they actually used.
Speaking transfer

Compare Two Photographs

Photo 1A small group of friends laughing around a table and sharing a meal, with no phones in sight.
Photo 2A person sitting alone on a sofa, lit by a phone screen and surrounded by takeaway containers.
Photo 3Two old friends hugging warmly in a doorway after time apart.

1. Compare two photographs. What might the people be feeling, and why does face-to-face contact matter here?

2. How might these different ways of connecting affect a person’s long-term happiness?

Partner follow-up: Do you think it is becoming harder for people to make genuine friends nowadays? Why / why not?
Candidate speaking time: about one minute. Partner follow-up: about 30 seconds.

In recent years, several governments (1) __________ begun treating loneliness as a public health issue rather (2) __________ a private misfortune. The UK, for example, appointed a minister (3) __________ responsibility for tackling social isolation, a move (4) __________ was widely reported around the world.
